How a tool gets reviewed

We sign up with our own card, run at least one real task through the tool — not the vendor's demo template — and note the setup time, the raw output, and anything that broke. Scores are assigned against a fixed rubric: setup time, output quality, pricing honesty after the trial period, and support response time. That rubric is on the homepage.

How a tool earns a "Recommended" stamp

A tool needs to score above 8/10 across the rubric and have no pricing or reliability issue we consider a dealbreaker. "Mixed" means it's genuinely useful with a specific, named limitation. "Skip" means we tested it and can't recommend it — those reviews exist too, they just don't carry affiliate links.

When we re-test

A review gets revisited when a tool changes its pricing, ships a major feature, or a reader flags something that contradicts our write-up. The date at the top of each review is the last time we verified it, not just when it was published.
